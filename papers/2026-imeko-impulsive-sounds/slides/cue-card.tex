{\large\bfseries Acoustic Detection of Impulsive Sounds}\hfill
{\small 10 min -- target \textbf{9:30} -- \textbf{4 clicks}}

\cue{0:00}{Title}{%
  \say{Good morning. My name is Martin Maxa, from the Department of Measurement here at
  this faculty. I worked on this together with Jakub Svatoš, from the same department.}
  \bt today: detection, classification, localization of impulsive sounds -- gunshots as
  the target class
  \bt \textbf{main focus: gunshot classification} -- cepstral features $+$ two
  classifiers; localization briefly}

\cue{0:45}{Motivation}{%
  \say{This work is part of the development of an acoustic sensor system for the
  protection of soft targets.}
  \bt complements cameras: \textbf{omnidirectional}, \textbf{no direct line of sight}
  \bt \textbf{[CUT]} complete system: detect, classify (to calibre), localize -- outdoors,
  noise, many reflections
  \bt \textbf{\color{accent}[CLICK]} \textbf{three questions:} (1) which cepstral
  representation separates gunshots from similar impulsive sounds, (2) NN or SVM,
  (3) frame length vs.\ reflections}

\cue{1:50}{Signature}{%
  \say{Let us first look at the signal of a gunshot. A gunshot has one or two components,
  depending on the projectile speed.}
  \bt \textbf{subsonic $\to$ muzzle blast only; supersonic $\to$ also a shock wave}
  \say{this recording is a supersonic shot, so we see both}
  \bt \textbf{shock wave} $\approx$2.5\,ms -- \textbf{[CUT]} along the trajectory, N-shaped
  pressure profile
  \bt \textbf{\color{accent}[CLICK]} \textbf{muzzle blast} $\approx$45\,ms later -- gases at
  the barrel, \emph{every gunshot}
  \bt between and after $=$ \textbf{reflections} -- \say{they matter for the choice of frame
  length, and I will return to them in the results}}

\cue{3:00}{Single-domain}{%
  \say{This also shows why one signal domain alone is not enough.}
  \bt spectrum $\to$ often the environment more than the gunshot
  \bt time-domain shape $\to$ changes during propagation, obstacles
  \bt $\Rightarrow$ features combining both domains -- cepstral features do exactly that}

\cue{3:30}{System}{%
  \say{Now let us look at the proposed system. It consists of several independent sensor
  units connected to a remote server.}
  \bt unit monitors continuously $\to$ on an impulsive event, sends the signal
  \bt server: \textbf{classification} $+$ \textbf{localization} of the source
  \bt several units: wider coverage $+$ position estimate $|$ tested with several
  calibres and ammunition types}

\cue{4:20}{Localize}{%
  \say{First, I will briefly explain the localization. Each sensor unit has two MEMS
  microphones, about twenty centimetres apart, sampled at 44.1 kilohertz.}
  \bt TDoA between the microphones $\to$ $\Theta = \arcsin\!\big(c\,\Delta n / (d f_s)\big)$
  $\to$ direction to the source
  \bt \textbf{\color{accent}[CLICK]} one unit $=$ one direction; two units $\to$ intersection
  $=$ position
  \bt \textbf{70 events}, 9 reference angles -- cross-corr $+$ parabolic interpolation,
  \textbf{MAE $0.77^\circ$}}

\cue{5:20}{Features}{%
  \say{Now I will move to the main part of the work: classification. We compared four
  different cepstral feature extractors.}
  \bt same chain: framing, FFT, \textbf{filter bank}, log, DCT $|$ \textbf{main difference
  $=$ the filter bank}, all four on the slide
  \bt \textbf{\color{accent}NEXT} (5:50) MFCC mel, dense low (from speech; works for gunshots)
  $|$ LFCC linear $|$ IMFCC inverted mel, dense high $|$ \textbf{\color{accent}GTCC gammatone},
  auditory model, more noise-robust $|$ (grid $4\times3\times2$ is on the slide)}

\cue{6:25}{\color{accent}TABLE $\to$ CHART}{%
  \say{This table shows the complete classification results for the nine-millimetre class.}
  \bt GTCC rows highlighted $|$ \emph{do not read the numbers} $\to$ \textbf{\color{accent}NEXT} (6:40)
  \say{This chart shows the same results, but only the Matthews correlation coefficient.}
  \bt \textbf{MCC:} whole confusion matrix, less affected by class imbalance
  \bt \textbf{red $=$ GTCC on top in both panels, every frame length} $|$ \textbf{NN better
  than SVM, difference grows with longer frames}
  \bt best \textbf{GTCC $+$ NN, 50\,ms $\to$ 99.15 \,/\, 96.77 \,/\, 0.98} $\Rightarrow$
  \textbf{117 of 118} test events correct (31 of them 9\,mm; 30 of 31 recalled)}

\cue{7:35}{Discussion}{%
  \say{From these results, we can make three main observations.}
  \bt MFCC, LFCC, IMFCC similar -- filter spacing makes little difference
  \bt \textbf{GTCC clearly better}, every frame length, both classifiers --
  \textbf{[CUT]} auditory model, noise-robust: \emph{possible} reason, not studied directly
  \bt \textbf{NN slightly better than SVM}, especially longer frames -- \emph{general trend,
  not every single result}
  \bt \textbf{\color{accent}[CLICK]} longer frames improve accuracy, \emph{but} 50\,ms already
  includes more reflections -- \say{so even longer frames would not necessarily help}}

\cue{8:45}{Conclusion}{%
  \say{To conclude.}
  \bt distributed sensor system $\to$ detection, classification, localization
  \bt cepstral features work well with both classifiers
  \bt GTCC best; NN slightly better than SVM, especially with longer frames
  \bt evaluated on real firearms, several calibres and ammunition types}

\cue{9:30}{}{\say{Thank you.}}

{\large\bfseries Acoustic Detection of Impulsive Sounds}\hfill
{\small 10 min --- target \textbf{9:30} --- 2 clicks}

\vspace{0.5mm}\hrule\vspace{2mm}

{\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}>{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{10mm} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{19mm} >{\raggedright\arraybackslash}p{147mm}@{}}

\cue{0:00}{Title}{%
  \say{Good morning. My name is Martin Maxa, from the Czech Technical University in
  Prague, Department of Measurement. I am presenting joint work with Doctor Jakub Svatoš.}
  \bt detection, classification \textbf{and localization} of impulsive sounds --- in
  practice, gunshots
  \bt \say{how we detect them, how we tell them apart, and how we find where they came from}}

\cue{0:30}{Why}{%
  \say{Shootings in public spaces are becoming more frequent, and gun ownership is
  rising worldwide.}
  \bt demand for autonomous security --- campuses, hospitals, parks, government buildings
  \bt cameras, drones, guards give \emph{some} protection --- fall short with an active shooter
  \bt \textbf{a camera has to be pointed the right way; sound does not care about line of sight}}

\cue{1:15}{Signature}{%
  \say{So what does a gunshot look like to a microphone? This is a real recording ---
  and the two components do not arrive together.}
  \bt \textbf{shock wave first}, $\approx$2.5\,ms --- only if $v>c$ --- bullet drags a cone
  of compressed air across the mic --- the N shape
  \bt \textbf{\color{accent}[CLICK]} muzzle blast $\approx$48\,ms, \emph{45\,ms later} ---
  gases leaving the barrel, 1--3\,ms, travels only at the speed of sound
  \bt between and after $=$ \textbf{reflections} --- \say{remember that, it comes back at the end}}

\cue{2:30}{Naive}{%
  \say{You might think this is easy --- just look at the spectrum, or just look at the
  waveform. Neither works on its own.}
  \bt spectrum alone $\to$ describes the environment, not the gunshot
  \bt time domain $\to$ distorted by nonlinear dispersion and obstacles
  \bt $\Rightarrow$ feature extraction must combine both domains}

\cue{3:00}{System}{%
  \say{Here is the system. Each sensor is a stand-alone unit.}
  \bt listens continuously $\to$ sends the detected signal to a remote server
  \bt server does two things: \textbf{classify} the event, and find \textbf{where} it was, in 2D
  \bt why several units? coverage --- and you need \emph{two bearings} for a position}

\cue{4:00}{Localize}{%
  \say{So how do we actually find the shooter?}
  \bt two MEMS mics per unit, $d = 0.186$\,m, $f_s = 44.1$\,kHz --- delay is at most
  $\approx$24 samples, \emph{but that is enough}
  \bt $\Theta = \arcsin\!\big(c\,\Delta n / (d f_s)\big)$ --- speed of sound $\times$ delay,
  over spacing $\times$ sampling rate
  \bt \textbf{\color{accent}[CLICK]} one unit $=$ a direction; two units cross $=$ the position
  \bt measured: cross-correlation $+$ parabolic interpolation, \textbf{MAE $0.77^\circ$},
  \textbf{70 events}, $-45^\circ$ to $+60^\circ$}

\cue{5:00}{Features}{%
  \say{Now the classification. The feature extractor is based on cepstral coefficients.}
  \bt same chain for all four: frame $\to$ FFT $\to$ power $\to$ log $\to$ DCT
  \bt \textbf{what differs is the filter bank, and it is not on the diagram}
  \bt MFCC (from speech, validated on ranges) $|$ LFCC linear $|$ IMFCC inverted $|$
  \textbf{\color{accent}GTCC gammatone $=$ models the ear}, more noise-resistant
  \bt tested: 4 extractors $\times$ 3 frame lengths (15/30/50\,ms) $\times$ 2 classifiers}

\cue{6:15}{\color{accent}TABLE}{%
  \say{This is the result table for the nine-millimetre class. Let me walk you through it.}
  \bt rows grouped by frame length, four extractors inside each $|$ columns $=$
  ACC, RLC, MCC --- each for SVM and NN
  \bt \textbf{\say{now look only at the red rows}} --- GTCC is the best row in every single block
  \bt best cell: \textbf{GTCC $+$ NN at 50\,ms $\to$ 99.15 \,/\, 96.77 \,/\, 0.98}
  \bt \textbf{context:} test set is 118 events, 31 of them 9\,mm $\Rightarrow$
  \textbf{117 of 118} correct, recall \textbf{30 of 31}}

\cue{7:45}{Numbers}{%
  \say{Three things come out of this.}
  \bt MFCC, LFCC, IMFCC almost identical --- reshaping a mel-type bank buys you little
  \bt \textbf{GTCC consistently better} --- gammatone resolves the low frequencies finely,
  and that is where muzzle-blast energy sits
  \bt \textbf{NN beats SVM}, and the gap grows with longer frames
  \bt longer frames do help, \emph{but} 50\,ms starts to include the room ---
  \textbf{the reflections from slide 3}}

\cue{8:45}{Conclusion}{%
  \say{To summarise.}
  \bt distributed network of stand-alone sensors $+$ server $\to$ detect, classify,
  localize in 2D
  \bt cepstral coefficients are an effective extractor, with both classifiers
  \bt GTCC beats the mel family; NN slightly better, especially at longer frames
  \bt validated with real firearms, several calibers and ammunition types}

\cue{9:30}{}{\say{Thank you --- I am happy to take questions.}}

# Retraining on the full HuggingFace set

2026-09-23. Run `full`, thirteen models: the two shipped ones as a baseline
and eleven retrained on 61 hours of audio instead of the 6 the previous models
saw. Detection on held-out audio goes from 42 % of drone windows to 95 % at the
same false-alarm budget. Then a real drone in front of the node's own
microphone inverted the ranking: every retrained model scored it as noise, and
the shipped model, with its threshold recalibrated, was the only one that
worked. The field section at the end is the part that matters.

## What changed, and why it was not the amount of data

The detector had a number that looked finished and a number that did not:
0.99 window AUC on the validation split, 0.7-0.94 on the unseen Halmstad
recordings. The usual reading is that the training set is too small. It was
also too small, but that gap was not measuring size.

`geronimobasso/drone-audio-detection-samples` is 180320 clips cut from longer
recordings by whoever compiled it: 163591 drone clips averaging 0.60 s and
16729 negatives averaging 7.28 s. The cut pieces are numbered with one running
index per class - `drone-95838.wav`, `drone-95839.wav` - and the numbering
follows the recordings. Neighbouring indices are neighbouring seconds of the
same audio.

The manifest grouped every clip on its own, so the split was drawn clip by
clip. The half-second before a cut trained the model and the half-second after
it tested the model. That is what 0.99 was measuring.

Measured rather than assumed. Taking runs of five consecutive indices and
comparing their log-spectra against clips from distant indices:

| | within a run | between distant runs | ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| negatives (shard 03) | 3.30 | 17.85 | **5.4x** |
| drones (shard 38) | 2.05 | 2.96 | **1.45x** |

The negatives are unambiguous. The drones look weaker only because every rotor
already resembles every other rotor, so spectral distance cannot separate them;
the numbering is the same.

The group is now a block of consecutive indices - 256 drone clips or 32
negatives, about three minutes of audio either way - and the split is drawn on
the group. 2999 groups, none of which straddles a split.

## The partition

    train 57 % | val 10 % | test 33 %   (by design)
    train 60.5 % | val 9.8 % | test 29.6 %   (as it fell, by hours)

Two thirds build the model, the remaining third judges it. Groups are not all
the same length, so the realised fractions drift a little from the nominal
ones; the hours above are what actually landed on each side.

The three-way split matters because the operating point is chosen, not given:
the threshold is the lowest value keeping false-alarm windows under 5 per hour
of negative audio. That choice is made on `val`. If `val` were the held-out
third, the threshold would be fitted to the same data the final numbers are
read from. `test` is touched by nothing - not a weight, not a threshold, not
the choice of which family to ship.

| split | clips | hours | groups |
|---|---|---|---|
| train | 104781 | 38.78 | 1700 |
| val | 20809 | 6.29 | 316 |
| test | 58062 | 18.99 | 983 |

Halmstad and Salford stay outside all three: public recordings of other drones
through other microphones, never trained on, which is the only evidence here
about a microphone the model has not heard.

## What was trained, and what was cut

Thirteen models are compared: the two shipped ones as they stand, and eleven
trained on the new partition.

| layout | extractor | width | families trained |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `stats` | 52 | `mlp`, `svm`, `gbt`, `mlp_reg`, `gbt_reg` |
| 2 | `stats_spectral` | 69 | the same five |
| 3 | `logmel` | 280 | `cnn_small` only |

Every model saw the same 489180 training windows, 163970 of them positive.

The layout-3 phase was cut short. `cnn_small` finished in 409 s; `cnn_ds` then
ran at 159 s per epoch, which is 106 minutes for its 40, with `cnn_1d` and
`cnn_wide` still behind it. It was stopped at epoch 20 of 40, where its holdout
loss was 0.136 against `cnn_small`'s final 0.217 - so a deeper convolutional
net clearly has headroom on this much data, and that is worth knowing even
though nothing was kept from the run.

Cutting it costs less than it looks. No CNN in this list can run on the board
as it stands: `cnn_small` is the cheapest of the four at 100816 MACs and was
measured at 9.93 ms per window on the M33 on 2026-09-07 - the architecture is
unchanged here, only its weights - against a 21.33 ms budget it shares
with the PDM conversion - an overrun that needs about a 3x speedup to close.
The heavier architectures are further away, not closer. They are a comparison
against models that cannot ship, and the hours they cost are better spent on
the families that can.

To finish them later:

```sh
bdtrain train --name full --layouts 3 --families cnn_ds cnn_1d cnn_wide
```

## Results

Every model's threshold is the lowest value keeping false-alarm windows under
5 per hour of negative audio on `val`, and is then applied unchanged
everywhere else. So the columns compare detection at a matched false-alarm
budget, not at each model's favourite operating point.

`drone %` is the share of positive windows called drone; `negatives %` the
share of negative windows correctly left alone. Both on `test`, the held-out
third.

### What got better

**Detection more than doubled.** `mlp_l2` calls 94.72 % of held-out drone
windows a drone where the shipped `mlp_v6` calls 42.35 %, at the same
false-alarm budget and a slightly lower false-alarm rate in practice. Six of
the retrained models clear 87 %.

**Salford went from a failure to a pass.** `mlp_l2` alarms on 9 of 9 flyovers;
`mlp_v6` alarms on 1. Salford is a genuinely unseen set - other drones,
another microphone, 50 kHz float - so this is generalisation, not fit.

**The synthetic stress probes stopped firing.** The failure the previous model
had - open hums read as rotors - is gone in every retrained model but one:
`mlp_v6` fires on 4.3 % of stress windows and `cnn_small` on 3.9 %, while every
retrained scikit-learn model fires on 0.0-0.9 %.

**The linear SVMs are finished.** Given 80 times the data, `svm_l1` and
`svm_l2` call essentially nothing a drone (0.00 % and 7.53 %). A linear margin
on these features cannot separate this much variety. They have been dropped
from the board's model set.

### What did not get better

**Halmstad went backwards.** `mlp_v6` holds 0.937 window AUC there and detects
7 of 21 drone clips; the best retrained model manages 0.792 AUC (`gbt_reg_l2`)
and 5 of 21 clips (`mlp_l2`). More data of this kind did not buy generalisation
to that microphone - it bought a sharper fit to the distribution the data comes
from. Halmstad and Salford disagreeing about which model generalises is itself
the finding: one unseen set is not a measure of generalisation.

**The node's own microphone got worse.** `auc_real` falls from 0.675
(`mlp_v6`) and 0.808 (`svm_v3`) to 0.17-0.63 across the retrained models. That
suite is 4 positive and 5 negative clips of speaker playback, which is far too
small to conclude from and is not a rotor, but it points the same way Halmstad
does.

The conclusion the previous report reached still stands, now with 61 hours
behind it rather than 6: **recordings made with the node's own microphone,
outdoors, of real drones, are the missing input.** No amount of this dataset
substitutes for them. The manifest already has a role for them (`real_mic`) and
the pipeline needs nothing else to use them.

## What is on the board now

`bdtrain export full --models mlp_l2 gbt_l2 gbt_reg_l2 cnn_small`, then the
registry. The image carries six models:

| | model | role |
|---|---|---|
| default | `mlp_v6` | unchanged weights; `default_thr_milli` **15000 -> 3000** (field section) |
| | `svm_v3` | unchanged |
| new | `mlp_l2` | best on public held-out audio, worst on the node's microphone |
| new | `gbt_l2` | best negatives-vs-detection balance of the trees on public audio |
| new | `gbt_reg_l2` | best Halmstad AUC of the retrained models |
| new | `cnn_small` | layout 3, comparison only - it overruns the window budget |

`svm_l1` and `svm_l2` were removed from the registry, the CMake list and
`models/`.

Host tests pass 10 of 10, model parity included; the registry test now has 85
checks. The firmware links at **RAM 20.94 %, FLASH 16.61 %** and is flashed on
the board at COM12.

Two things in the C tree changed on the way, both worth knowing about:

* `boomdetect_init` used to take `boomdetect_extractor_default()` - the first
  extractor in the table, layout 1 - whenever the caller left `cfg.extractor`
  NULL. The moment a layout-2 model was made the default, init refused it,
  including through its own NULL-classifier fallback. It now takes
  `boomdetect_extractor_for_layout(model->layout_id)`. This is the library-level
  form of the `detect_service.c` bug found on 2026-09-07; fixing it there had
  left every other caller free to repeat it.
* `registry_test.c` asserted that layout `MEAN_STD_DMEAN_CMAX + 1` is foreign.
  That silently became the valid layout 2 when `stats_spectral` shipped, so the
  test was asserting that a valid model is rejected. It now uses an id no
  extractor claims, `REQUIRE`s that premise, and adds two checks that a named
  extractor and the model must agree on layout.

## Reproducing this

```sh
cd fw/common/boomdetect/training
bdtrain fetch                     # 39 shards, 6.81 GB, resumable
bdtrain manifest                  # 183785 clips, 64.06 h
bdtrain features hf_drone_audio --force
bdtrain train --name full --layouts 1 2 --families mlp svm gbt mlp_reg gbt_reg
bdtrain train --name full --layouts 3 --families cnn_small
bdtrain compare full
bdtrain export full --models mlp_l2 gbt_l2 gbt_reg_l2 cnn_small
```

Wall clock on a 20-core desktop, measured: 5.7 min to fetch, 0.6 min to
enumerate, 6.1 min of front end, 3.4 min for the ten scikit-learn models, 6.8
min for `cnn_small`, 2.0 min to compare - about 25 minutes end to end, of which
the download is the largest single piece and is only paid once.

## Field test: a real drone, the node's own microphone

The same evening, seven 30 s recordings from the board (48 kHz PCM16, mic slot
A), `boomdetect-data/raw/field/2026-09-23/`: `01_room`, `02_motors_ground`,
`03_hover_2m`, `04_throttle_sweep`, `05_move`, `06_hover_far`, `07_confusers`
(speech, claps, keyboard, appliances). `bdtrain score REC.wav` is new: it
replays a recording through the board's chain - 3x decimation, the same MFCC
tables, the same per-frame gate - and prints every model's decision per
window next to the eight spectral scalars, so a score can be traced to the
features that produced it.

### The microphone carries the rotor

`02_motors_ground` shows a textbook comb at **211 / 422 / 633 / 850 Hz**; the
scorer's f0 estimate is 209.9 Hz. Hover sits around 250 Hz. 40-50 % of the
drone's energy is above 4 kHz. The room has 0.02 % above 4 kHz and every frame
of it is below squelch. Whatever is wrong is downstream of the microphone.

Every take carries a ~0.12 s clipped transient at the start (PDM settling) and
a DC offset of +0.005; trim 0.2 s before training on them.

### The public ranking inverted

Median decision per model, hover at 2 m against the confusers:

| model | layout | hover 2 m | confusers | trained on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **mlp_v6** (shipped) | 1 | **+2.4** | **-43.1** | ~6 h, August |
| svm_v3 (shipped) | 1 | +0.8 | -0.8 | ~6 h, August |
| gbt_reg_l2 | 2 | -1.0 | -3.6 | 61 h, today |
| cnn_small | 3 | -2.2 | -11.9 | 61 h, today |
| mlp_reg_l1 | 1 | -3.1 | -15.7 | 61 h, today |
| mlp_l1 | 1 | -7.3 | -17.7 | 61 h, today |
| gbt_l2 | 2 | -2.3 | -7.8 | 61 h, today |
| **mlp_l2** | 2 | **-47.7** | -27.4 | 61 h, today |

`mlp_l2` - 94.7 % on the public held-out third - is *more* certain the drone is
noise than it is about the empty room. `mlp_l1`, the same 51-32-1 network as
`mlp_v6` trained on eighty times the data, lands at -7.3 where `mlp_v6` lands at
+2.4. Same architecture, more data, worse on the drone in the room.

The mechanism is visible in the scalars. On this rig the drone has
`hi_ratio` 0.5-0.65 and `harmonicity` 0.04; the room has 0.007 and 0.14. The
layout-2 features point the opposite way from what the HuggingFace set taught,
which is why layout 2 fails harder than layout 1 (`mlp_reg_l2` -55.8,
`mlp_reg_l1` -3.1). The throttle-sweep hypothesis - that the blade-pass
frequency leaves the 60-392 Hz comb range - was wrong: f0 stayed 222-284 Hz
through the sweep.

The likely reason the August model transfers: its 6 hours were dominated by
Bebop and Mambo recorded indoors with a close microphone, which is acoustically
closer to a small quad at 2 m in a room than the average over 163 591 clips
from six other sources. More data pulled the boundary toward the average public
drone and away from this one. The leak found in the morning inflated the old
model's *number*; it did not spoil the old *model*.

### What works: mlp_v6 at 3.0

Share of windows over the threshold; in brackets, positions where the board's
2-of-4 rule would alarm:

```
thr     motors  hover_2m  sweep     move      far       confusers
15.0      0%      2% [ 0]   0% [ 0]   5% [ 0]   0% [ 0]    0% [ 0]   <- as shipped
 3.0      0%     41% [33]  32% [24]  47% [37]  33% [29]    0% [ 0]
 1.0      2%     70% [56]  52% [42]  58% [46]  53% [44]    2% [ 0]
```

`svm_v3` at 0.5 fires on 27 % of confuser windows. Nothing else separates at
any threshold. `mlp_v6`'s threshold was lowered to 3.0 and flashed; it is the
default again. One drone, one room, two and a half minutes: a calibration, not
a validation.

Earlier the same evening, `detect` with squelch 0 and a threshold of -15 called
silence a drone. The features are level-invariant, so a floor-only frame has a
"shape" the model has never seen; that is what the squelch gate is for. Never
read verdicts from a squelch-0 run.

### What this changes

The public sets alone select the wrong model. The next training run has to
include the node's own recordings - the seven takes above are the first ones,
and the manifest's `real_mic` role is where they go - and the winner has to be
chosen on how it does on this rig, with the public suites demoted to a check
that nothing broke elsewhere. Whether that is a fine-tune of `mlp_v6`, a
weighted mix, or a per-rig calibration layer is the next question; the data to
answer it now exists.
